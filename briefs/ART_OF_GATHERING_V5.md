# HUBB — The Art of the Gathering

Packaging design V5 · 5 September 2026

**حُبّ يجمعنا — HUBB brings us together.**

**There’s always room for one more.**

## The selected creative direction

The user’s artistic HUBB reference is now the visual benchmark: expressive Arabic calligraphy, a diagonal painted flavour stroke, fine woven geometric detail and appetizing in-shell sunflower seeds. The new range uses a warm ivory base with roasted-brown lettering. Black and charcoal packaging fields are excluded, including on the family bucket and retail display.

The reference guides the artwork. It does not add its Matcha flavour, 100 g size, stand-up pouch structure, fingerprint graphic or product-performance statements to this range. The four launch flavours remain Classic Sea Salt, Garlic Salt, Pepper Lime and Fire Salt.

This V5 direction supersedes the unpublished geometric Gathering Weave exploration. The V3 website remains available as the earlier concept for comparison.

## Why this feels connected

HUBB’s connection to Saudi life is the shared moment: a parked road-trip stop, the break between games, a family film and the conversation that carries on. The small sachet gives each person a choice; the cup and family bucket bring those choices together. The paper shell bag expresses consideration for the people and place around you.

Arabic lettering leads the identity. Painterly movement gives it a human gesture. Fine geometric rhythm adds cultural texture. The seed imagery makes the food unmistakable. Together these create a proposed contemporary Saudi brand language without inventing Saudi origins for the crop.

NOUG’s camel-milk identity is a useful example of connecting a product with meaningful local heritage and modern everyday use, as described by [its packaging supplier SIG](https://www.sig.biz/en-ae/media/press-releases/sig-enters-a-new-product-category-with-camel-milk-launch-in-sig-aseptic-cartons). HUBB applies that principle to gathering. NOUG’s camel imagery, logo and packaging layouts are not reused.

[UNESCO describes Al Sadu](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-weaving-of-al-sadu-02158) through rhythmic geometric repetition and shared tradition. That is cultural context for the woven visual rhythm here. The pattern in these mockups is a newly interpreted printed design, not a reproduction of an authenticated historical motif or evidence of an artisan partnership. Al Sadu is shared regional heritage, including Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Qatar.

## One artistic grammar for every pack

1. **Warm ivory ground.** The pack should feel light and tactile. Paper-like texture is a visual treatment; the sachet’s actual barrier material is selected with the converter.
2. **Expressive Arabic wordmark.** Flowing brush-calligraphic حُبّ, with the small seed accent and a widely spaced HUBB companion. Use one approved drawing across every SKU and format. The principal lettering is roasted brown rather than black.
3. **Painted flavour sweep.** One broad diagonal brush stroke with tonal variation, identical angle and placement across the range. It is printed artwork, not an assumed physical paint treatment.
4. **Fine woven linework.** A restrained pale-sand pattern of connected geometric forms follows the painted stroke. Preserve breathing room around the logo and flavour.
5. **Appetizing product illustration.** A small group of striped in-shell sunflower seeds and an opened shell with a golden kernel anchors the lower panel. One relevant flavour ingredient appears alongside it.
6. **Quiet information footer.** Flavour, product category, sachet count and net weight stay readable. Grams are never a hero graphic.

Keep the pattern composition, logo drawing, illustration scale, flavour placement and label spacing consistent. Flavour colour and ingredient cue change; the brand architecture does not.

## Four flavours

| Flavour | Arabic creative identification | Painted colour family | Ingredient cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic Sea Salt | ملح بحري | Indigo and cobalt blue | Salt crystals |
| Garlic Salt | ملح بالثوم | Marigold and saffron yellow | Garlic clove |
| Pepper Lime | فلفل ولايم | Fresh olive and lime green | Lime and black pepper |
| Fire Salt | ملح حار | Chilli red and terracotta | Red chilli |

Common ground: warm ivory. Common main lettering: roasted umber. Common fine detail: pale sand. Screen colours and rendered textures are creative targets, not final print specifications. Retain both words and ingredient cues so flavour identification does not depend on colour alone.

## Formats and contents

| Format | Contents | Net seed contents | Visual application |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual sachet | One sealed sachet of one flavour | 30 g | Small pillow sachet; one calligraphic identity, one painted flavour stroke. |
| Journey Cup | Five sealed 30 g sachets of one flavour and one separate paper shell bag | 150 g | Tapered popcorn-style paper cup; same ivory artwork adapted to the curved wrap. |
| Family Mix | One bucket containing twenty sealed sachets, five of each flavour; four personal shell bags proposed | 600 g | Ivory bucket with brown calligraphy, fine sand linework and pictures of the four sachets on the front. No multicolour outer brushstroke. |
| Retail display | Twenty-four individually saleable 30 g sachets of one flavour | 720 g across the display | Ivory printed board with matching flavour stroke; open front exposes the sachets. |

**Sachet footer:** Net wt. 30 g.

**Cup footer:** 5 × 30 g sachets · Net wt. 150 g. Small supporting line: Sachets + shell bag.

**Family footer:** 4 flavours · 20 × 30 g sachets · Net wt. 600 g.

**Display:** 24 individually saleable sachets. Its 720 g total must not appear as the weight of an individual pack.

The cup contains sachets rather than 150 g of loose seeds. The family format is one larger bucket, not four separate cups. Its four printed sachet pictures identify the flavours inside; colour is confined to these pictures, with an ivory-and-brown outer design. The lid also stays ivory with restrained sand detail. Product-lineup images may show one sachet beside each cup to compare artwork; that is a design comparison, not a complete contents layout.

Cup and bucket dimensions must come from a physical packing trial using filled 30 g sachets, closures and the selected accessories. No universal car-holder fit is asserted. The drawing area for a curved cup must follow the supplier’s wrap geometry.

## The shell bag belongs to the experience

Use a separate small gusseted paper bag, with a simple shell symbol and understated brown lettering: **للقشور / SHELLS HERE**. It stays visually distinct from the food packaging. Store it clean and folded apart from loose food. Four personal bags are proposed for the family format, subject to the final component decision.

For the cup ritual, remove the sealed sachets and bag, open one sachet and keep the others sealed. The empty cup may serve the opened seeds after its food-contact construction has been confirmed. Empty shells go in the separate bag. Road-trip photography uses parked or passenger settings, with no faces shown as requested.

## Retail, convenience and ecommerce

**Traditional trade / baqala:** make the single sachet easy to pick from the open display. Large Arabic branding, colour and recognizable seeds must communicate quickly at actual size.

**Petrol-station convenience:** group the four Journey Cup flavours together. Show the paper bag and the sealed-sachet contents in a secondary explanation near the display, keeping the primary product front clean.

**Supermarket / hypermarket:** place the family bucket near sharing occasions and use single-flavour sachet displays for individual purchase. The family bucket remains ivory, using pictures of the four sachets to explain the assortment clearly.

**Ecommerce / delivery apps:** lead with the sold pack on a clear background. Follow with a contents image, the flavour choice and a close view of the artwork. Retailer names or logos do not appear as claims of existing distribution.

The retail imagery is a proposed placement. No retailer partnership, price, availability, conversion uplift or market-share result is asserted.

## What to carry into final artwork

The image-generation workflow makes the new artistic direction visible across packaging. It can introduce small lettering, repeated-pattern and product-count variations. The contents table and typeset text in this brief remain the source of truth. Create one final vector Arabic wordmark, one pattern master and one product-illustration master before making supplier-specific artwork files.

Review the creative Arabic with a Saudi lettering/copy specialist. If a craft collaboration is commissioned, credit the actual practitioner. Neither a collaborator nor a heritage endorsement is represented as already appointed.

To judge the design, show the 30 g sachet at printed size, the range from shelf distance and an ecommerce image at phone size. Ask Arabic-speaking shoppers what product and flavour they see and what the design reminds them of before explaining the story. Emotional connection and purchase appeal are hypotheses to test, not guaranteed outcomes of the artwork.

This is a developed creative mockup system, not print-ready manufacturing artwork. The design-critique workflow informed hierarchy and consistency; the built-in image-generation tool produced the realistic images.
